SPEAKER 3 · 8:30–9:20 “Now, lots of things are warm: a laptop charger, a cup of tea, a sunny wall. If the robot raised an alarm for every warm thing, rescuers would stop trusting it. So it never relies on one reading. It collects four clues and adds them into a score out of a hundred. Clue one, heat contrast: how much warmer than the room — but never hotter than forty-two degrees, because nothing alive is hotter than that. Clue two, size: a person covers several pixels; a cup of tea covers one. Clue three — our favourite — warmer when closer. As the robot creeps forward, a real person fills more and more of its view, so the reading goes up. A reflection or a distant warm wall doesn’t behave like that. Clue four, movement, from the motion sensor. Sixty or more: survivor alert. Thirty-five to fifty-nine: logged as possible, for a human to check. Below that: rejected.” [CLICK]
