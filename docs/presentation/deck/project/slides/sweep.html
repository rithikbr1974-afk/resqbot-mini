SPEAKER 3 · 7:00–7:50 “Thank you, [Speaker 2]. A real thermal camera costs thousands of rupees. We used something much simpler: an infrared thermometer — the same idea as the forehead thermometers you saw everywhere during COVID. It costs seven hundred and fifty-nine rupees, and it only gives ONE number: the temperature of whatever it’s pointing at. So we made it move. Two hobby servo motors swing it from left to right in fifteen steps, at two heights — once looking at the floor, once looking straight ahead. Every cell is read twice and averaged. Fifteen times two: a thirty-pixel heat picture of the room, every five seconds or so. [Point to the warm cells.] Blue is the room. Yellow and orange is something warmer — here, a person lying slightly to the right. Thirty pixels doesn’t sound like much, but it’s enough to answer the two questions that matter: is there a person, and which way do I turn?” [CLICK]
